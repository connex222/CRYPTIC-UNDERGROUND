CRYPTIC UNDERGROUND

How cryptic crosswords work

A cryptic clue normally gives you two routes to the same answer: a definition and wordplay. The definition is usually at the beginning or end; the wordplay is the mechanism that builds the answer.

1. Find the definition

Look at either end of the clue. The definition should be a fair synonym, description or example of the answer. It normally does not sit in the middle.

2. Solve the wordplay

The remaining words should account for every letter. Good cryptics are precise: the wordplay is a recipe, not merely a vague hint.

Anagram

Letters are rearranged. Indicators include mixed, broken, wild, confused, working. Example: Rearranged rate (4) → TEAR.

Charade

Short pieces are placed together. Example: Girl + article can produce a longer answer by joining two smaller components.

Container

One set of letters goes inside another. Watch for around, holding, containing, embracing, wearing.

Reversal

Letters are written backwards. Across clues often use back, returned, reversed; Down clues can use up, rising, going north.

Hidden answer

The answer appears consecutively inside the clue, usually signalled by in, hiding, concealed, part of. Ignore spaces and punctuation when searching.

Initial / final letters

Indicators such as initially, starts, heads can mean take first letters; finally, ends, tails can mean take final letters.

Alternate letters

Oddly, regularly, alternately may tell you to take every other letter. Check exactly which positions the clue calls for.

Deletion

Indicators such as without, losing, dropping, heartless tell you to remove letters. The wording should make clear what is removed.

Homophone

The answer sounds like another word. Look for heard, reportedly, in conversation, they say. Spelling may differ.

Double definition

Two separate meanings point to the same word. These are often short and deceptively simple, with no letter-building mechanism.

Cryptic definition

The whole clue gives a witty or misleading description rather than a conventional definition plus mechanical wordplay. These can be harder to prove.

Abbreviations

Cryptics use established shorthand: N/S/E/W for compass points, H for hour, R for right, Roman numerals, musical notes and many conventional crossword abbreviations.

Crossing letters

Never ignore the grid. A few checked letters can transform a difficult clue. The answer must satisfy both the clue and every crossing entry.

Enumeration

The number in brackets tells you the answer length. For example, (5) means five letters. A phrase may be shown as (3,4).

The grandmaster test

  1. Definition is exact enough to be defensible.
  2. Wordplay accounts for every letter.
  3. Indicators clearly signal the operation.
  4. Grammar and tense agree with the answer.
  5. The clue has one intended solution once crossers are considered.
  6. The surface should read naturally without sacrificing fairness.

Editorial note: obviously broken pseudo-wordplay variants are removed where a sound alternative already exists. This is a conservative cleanup, not a claim that every remaining clue has received full human setter-level verification.

Difficulty is graded from the construction of the clue: Easy favours direct definitions, double definitions and simple wordplay; Medium requires a clear construction or several steps; Hard uses layered mechanisms, less obvious indicators, tougher transformations or more demanding vocabulary.
